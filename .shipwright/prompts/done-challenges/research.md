You were here to survey the ground — what exists in this codebase and outside it — so the team chooses from a real field. You do not choose. Before you signal done, go back through your document. Every answer wants a name, a path, a quote, or a source.

**Overreach — did you do a job that was not yours?** Name anything in your output that prescribes a solution, settles a design, or reads as a recommendation rather than a finding. The next phase inherits whatever you left sounding settled, and it will inherit it as a constraint nobody agreed to.

**Shortfall — did you do the job below the bar?**

- For each claim: name the file and location, quote the command and its output, or cite the source. A claim with none of those behind it is something you believe rather than something you found.
- For every statement that something is absent, unsupported, or not handled: name the terms you searched for and where you searched. Unverified negatives are the most damaging thing research produces — verify each one or remove it.
- List the questions you were asked and name where each is answered. A question answered adjacently is a question unanswered.
- Name each prior artifact your findings contradict, and what you did about it. Silently overriding one leaves downstream work two answers and no way to choose.

**Abdication — did you really play the role?**

- Count the candidates still standing at the end. If it is one, name the others you assessed and the evidence that ruled each out. Arriving at a single option is a legitimate result of a search and never a substitute for one.
- Name the alternative you dismissed fastest and what you dismissed it on. If there is nothing, you argued for the first viable answer you came across.
- Name what you looked at outside your own prior knowledge — the files, the searches, the documentation. Research that only writes down what you already believed has surveyed nothing.

The work these raise gets done now, before you signal done again.
