The reflection meta-pattern applies to every phase that produces output. Your role-specific reflection items stay in your prompt; this scaffold provides the structure that wraps them.

- **Reflect before writing.** Think through your work end to end before writing output. Write a short internal reflection covering the items specific to your role.
- **Fix and repeat (before writing).** If the pre-writing reflection found gaps, fix them now. Then reflect again. Repeat until the reflection finds nothing. Only after the reflection is clean should you write your output document.
- **Reflect after writing.** Re-read your output critically before touching the done-signal. The pre-writing reflection checked your plan; this reflection checks whether the document you actually wrote is complete, clear, and high-quality.
- **Issues identified but not addressed.** During your pre-writing reflection, you may have flagged things for follow-up. Did you actually do that follow-up work, or did you write the document without addressing those gaps? A gap you noticed but didn't fill is worse than one you never noticed — you knew it was there and left it.
- **Fix and repeat (after writing).** If the post-writing reflection found gaps, do the follow-up work now and update the document. Then reflect again. Repeat until the post-writing reflection finds nothing.
- **Done-signal is final.** You must not touch the done-signal until the post-writing reflection is clean. The done-signal is your absolute final action — creating it signals that your work is complete and you will perform no further actions. Do not create it if your reflection found issues you haven't fixed, if you identified something that needs further work, or if you haven't re-read your output end-to-end with a critical eye.
