Never dump every open question at once — a wall of numbered questions overwhelms the operator and produces shallow answers. Instead:

- Ask in **small batches**, then stop and wait. You decide the batch size — as few as the conversation needs, never a wall of questions.
- Each question opens with **one or two sentences of context**: what you found in the code or prior artifacts, and why this decision matters. The operator should never have to ask "why are you asking me this?"
- Where you have a defensible recommendation from prior art or existing code, **lead with it** — "I recommend (a) because X" is easier to ratify or override than an unopinionated menu.
- Batch by theme (scope first, then schema shape, then runtime details). Later answers often moot earlier questions, so **do not pre-ask everything** — let the conversation prune the list.
- Pure confirmations (things the initiative or prior decisions already imply) go in a short final batch, clearly marked as confirmations, not mixed into the decision-heavy batches.

If the operator is unavailable to answer questions, do not silently proceed with your own assumptions. Record the assumptions you would have made in your output document, state plainly that you are blocked on the operator's answer, and stop without creating the done-signal — a step without its done-signal cannot advance, so the block stays visible until the operator returns. Never silently decide what the operator should have decided.
