## Background/Context

OCHRE: `attic_height = sqrt(attic_gable_area * tan(roof_tilt))`. This derives from
the roof geometry. A hardcoded 1.5m would be wrong for most houses (typical US attic
heights range from 1.2m to 3.5m depending on pitch and span).

## Investigation Instructions

1. **Read `building.rs` `compute_attic_volume()`** (lines ~1838-1901):
   - Is `attic_height` derived from gable area and roof tilt?
   - Or is it hardcoded?
   - How is gable area determined (which surfaces)?

2. **Read OCHRE `parse_hpxml_zones()`** (line ~617):
   - Document the exact formula.
   - How does OCHRE handle 1 vs 2+ gable walls?

3. **Compare formulas.**
4. **Check:** is `roof_tilt` from HPXML pitch correctly converted to radians for `tan()`?


## Findings

### Formula — Correct

Both HARES and OCHRE use the same triangular-prism geometry:

```
tan(roof_tilt) = height / (width / 2)
gable_area ≈ 0.5 × width × height  →  height = √(gable_area × tan(tilt))
attic_volume = 0.5 × floor_area × height
```

Attic height is **not hardcoded**. It is computed from gable wall area and roof tilt
at `building.rs:1942`.

Pitch-to-tilt conversion is parity-equivalent: OCHRE uses `atan(pitch/12)` in
`pitch2deg()` (`units.py:26`); HARES uses `(pitch / 12.0).atan().to_degrees()`
(`building.rs:936`). Identical.

---

### F1 — SEVERITY: HIGH — 3-gable area selection: sorted median vs. OCHRE unsorted index 1

**Location:** `building.rs:1931–1936`

HARES sorts the gable areas and picks `sorted[1]` (the median). OCHRE line 609
picks `attic_wall_areas[1]` — the **second entry in the original, unsorted list**,
which is the second attic gable wall as parsed from the XML.

The sorted `low, med, high` block in OCHRE (lines 610–611) is only used to compute
`third_gable_area`, not `attic_gable_area`. When the garage gable area happens to
fall numerically between the two attic gable areas, HARES's median will pick the
garage gable instead of an attic gable, producing a wrong attic height.

**Fix:** Separate gable walls by zone adjacency type (Attic↔Outdoor vs.
Attic↔Garage) and select `attic_gable_area` only from the Attic↔Outdoor subset,
matching OCHRE's intent.

---

### F2 — SEVERITY: HIGH — Adjacent Attic Wall and Attic Garage Wall excluded from gable collection

**Location:** `building.rs:1898–1907`

The gable wall filter requires `exterior_zone == Outdoor | None`. This silently
drops two categories OCHRE includes:

**a) "Adjacent Attic Wall" (Attic ↔ Attic):** In multi-unit buildings a wall
between two attic spaces has `ExteriorAdjacentTo=attic`. HARES drops it because the
exterior zone parses as `ZoneType::Attic`, not `Outdoor`.

**b) "Attic Garage Wall" (Attic ↔ Garage):** OCHRE lines 583–596 have a dedicated
branch: when Attic↔Garage walls are present it pools Attic Wall + Adjacent Attic
Wall + Attic Garage Wall areas and picks `max(areas[0], areas[1])`. HARES has no
equivalent; such walls are absent from the gable list entirely.

The Attic Garage Wall case is uncommon in standard ResStock inputs but Adjacent
Attic Walls appear in multi-family HPXML files.

---

### F3 — SEVERITY: LOW — No warning on asymmetric 2-gable pair

**Location:** `building.rs:1929–1930`

OCHRE asserts both gable areas agree within 0.2 m² (`hpxml.py:604`). HARES silently
picks `gable_areas[0]` with no validation. An L-shaped or asymmetric attic parsed as
two gables of very different sizes would produce a wrong height with no diagnostic.

**Fix:** Add a `tracing::warn!` when the two areas differ by more than 0.2 m².

## Recommended Tests

1. Unit test for `compute_attic_volume` with a 2-gable symmetric case (pitch=6/12,
   gable=10 m², floor=80 m²) — assert volume matches `0.5 × 80 × √(10 × tan(atan(0.5)))`.
2. Unit test for the 3-gable case with two equal attic gables and one smaller garage
   gable — assert the attic gables, not the garage gable, determine the height.
3. Integration test with a multi-family HPXML that contains an Adjacent Attic Wall —
   assert volume is non-None and matches expected geometry.
