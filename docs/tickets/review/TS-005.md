## Background/Context

Zone temperature response depends on the ratio of air mass to thermal mass (furniture,
interior walls, floor slab). Air-only zones respond instantly to heating/cooling; zones
with thermal mass have inertia. EnergyPlus models zone air and "internal mass" as
separate nodes. OCHRE does the same via its RC network.

If HARES only models zone air without interior thermal mass, temperature will
over-respond to disturbances, and HVAC cycling behavior will be wrong.

## Investigation Instructions

1. **Read HARES RC network construction:**
   - Does the zone air node have only air thermal capacity? Or does it include
     furniture/interior wall mass?
   - Are interior walls and furniture modeled as separate RC paths (with their own
     nodes) that exchange heat with zone air via convection?
   - What is the zone air thermal capacity? `ρ * Cp * Volume`?

2. **Read OCHRE interior mass model:**
   - How does OCHRE add furniture and interior walls to the RC network?
   - Do they have separate temperature nodes?
   - What are the convective film coefficients between mass and air?

3. **Compare zone temperature response:**
   - For a step change in heating input (e.g., 1000W added to zone), how fast does
     HARES zone temp respond vs OCHRE?
   - Is the time constant similar? (τ = RC)

4. **Check thermal mass properties:**
   - What R-value and capacitance are assigned to furniture?
   - What R-value and capacitance for interior walls?
   - Compare with OCHRE defaults.

5. **Check longwave radiation exchange:**
   - Do interior mass surfaces participate in longwave radiation exchange?
   - ENVELOPE-008 is about splitting LWR between RC node and zone air — check status.

## Findings

### Q1: Zone air node — air-only or with mass?

**ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE (matches OCHRE design)**

The zone air node in HARES carries a capacitance of `ρ_air × Cp_air × Volume × INTERIOR_MASS_MULTIPLIER` where `INTERIOR_MASS_MULTIPLIER = 7.0`.

Code: `crates/hares-envelope/src/boundary_rc.rs:25,233`

```rust
pub const INTERIOR_MASS_MULTIPLIER: f64 = 7.0;
// ...
(AIR_DENSITY_KG_M3 * AIR_CP_J_KG_K * volume * INTERIOR_MASS_MULTIPLIER).max(MIN_CAPACITANCE_J_K)
```

This is an inflated zone-air capacitance, NOT a pure air mass. The multiplier accounts for the fact that air in the zone exchanges heat quickly with nearby lightweight objects (duct surfaces, thin furnishings, etc.), making the effective thermal mass of "zone air" larger than pure air. OCHRE applies the identical formula at `Envelope.py:445-446` using its `Thermal Mass Multiplier` (default 7).

Per the OCHRE documentation (`ModelingApproach.rst:110-118`): "a multiplier is generally applied to this capacitance... Values for this multiplier in the literature range from 3-15 depending on the study. OCHRE uses a default multiplier of 7."

### Q2: Are interior walls and furniture modeled as separate RC nodes?

**ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE (matches OCHRE design)**

Yes. HARES generates two classes of same-zone boundaries:

1. **Interior Wall** — auto-generated in `crates/hares-io/src/hpxml/building.rs:572-603`. Area = conditioned floor area. LUT name = `"Interior Wall"`. Material: gypsum board + wall stud/cavity + gypsum board (3 layers, ~1.37 kJ/m²·K total capacitance per OCHRE materials LUT).

2. **Furniture** — auto-generated in `crates/hares-io/src/hpxml/building.rs:605-649`. Default fraction = 0.4 × floor area for conditioned zone. LUT names: `"Indoor Furniture"`, `"Foundation Furniture"`, `"Garage Furniture"`. Material: 0.1524 m thick, density 640.8 kg/m³, Cp = 1214 J/kg·K (wood composite, ~118.6 kJ/m²·K total capacitance per OCHRE materials LUT).

Both are same-zone (`interior_zone == exterior_zone == Conditioned`), so `ExteriorTarget::Zone(same_idx)`. In `assemble_building_rc`, the `same_zone = true` path applies: RC layers are halved (only the inner half is kept as a dead-end thermal fin connected to zone air by film resistance), and no exterior connection is made. This matches OCHRE's `create_rc_data` halving logic (`envelope.py:308-337`).

The RC nodes for these boundaries are **separate state-vector entries** from the zone air node, with their own temperature state and connection to zone air through film resistance. This is not a lumped approach.

### Q3: Zone air thermal capacity formula

**ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE (matches OCHRE)**

`C_zone = ρ_air × Cp_air × Volume × 7.0`

For a typical 185 m² floor area at 2.5 m height (462.5 m³):
- Pure air: 462.5 × 1.2041 × 1006 = ~559,000 J/K
- With 7× multiplier: ~3,913,000 J/K

The furniture RC nodes add additional mass on top of this. For a 185 m² conditioned zone:
- Interior wall mass: 185 m² × ~1370 J/(m²·K) ≈ 253,450 J/K
- Furniture mass: 74 m² (0.4 × 185) × ~118,500 J/(m²·K) ≈ 8,769,000 J/K

So the furniture RC node capacitance dominates. Combined system: zone air (3.9 MJ/K) + furniture fin (8.8 MJ/K) + interior wall (0.25 MJ/K) ≈ 12.9 MJ/K effective thermal mass.

### Q4: OCHRE comparison

**ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE — architectures are identical**

OCHRE uses the exact same two-level structure (`Envelope.py:938-968`, `load_rc_data`):
1. Zone air node gets `C_LIV = volume × ρ × Cp × 7` (7× multiplier via `Thermal Mass Multiplier` key).
2. Interior Wall and Furniture boundaries each create their own RC capacitor nodes from the OCHRE material LUT via `boundary.capacitors`. These are additive, not substitutes.
3. Both sets are loaded together: `capacitances.update(**boundary.capacitors)` (adds furniture/wall nodes), then `capacitances.update({zone.label: zone.capacitance ...})` (adds zone air node).

HARES mirrors this architecture exactly. The OCHRE documentation explicitly states: "Partition walls and furniture are modeled explicitly with surface areas and material properties like any other surface and exchange heat through both convection and radiation." The 7× multiplier is separate from and additive to the explicit RC mass nodes.

### Q5: Interior mass surfaces in longwave radiation exchange

**ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE (matches OCHRE)**

Yes. Same-zone boundaries with `is_conditioned_interior = true` are included in interior LWR.

Code path in `crates/hares-core/src/dwelling/solver_builder.rs:602-666`:

```rust
let include_in_lwr = if is_window {
    sb.is_exterior && sb.area_m2 > 0.0
} else {
    sb.is_conditioned_interior && sb.area_m2 > 0.0
};
```

Since furniture and interior walls have `interior_zone = Conditioned` and `is_conditioned_interior = true`, they are included in the `surfaces_by_zone` map and end up in `thermal_cfg.interior_lwr_zones`. They receive a `state_index` pointing to their inner RC node (via `inner_wiring`) so their surface temperature is tracked separately from zone air during LWR exchange.

OCHRE does the same: `Zone.create_surfaces()` at `Envelope.py:517-558` picks up `b.int_surface` for all boundaries connecting to the zone (including Interior Wall and Furniture), and these surfaces participate in the `s_view_factors` LWR calculation.

### Q6: INTERIOR_MASS_MULTIPLIER = 7.0 — multiplier on air capacitance vs separate RC node

**ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE**

`INTERIOR_MASS_MULTIPLIER = 7.0` in HARES is applied **exclusively to the zone air node capacitance**. It is not a separate RC node. The formula `ρ_air × Cp_air × Volume × 7` results in a single lumped capacitor for the zone air state.

Separate RC nodes for furniture and interior walls exist alongside this inflated zone air node. The multiplier and the explicit mass nodes serve different physical purposes:
- The 7× multiplier captures fast-exchange thermal mass (duct surfaces, thin material surfaces) and the mixing lag of HVAC conditioning.
- The furniture and interior wall RC nodes capture slow-exchange thermal mass with explicit conduction time constants.

This correctly mirrors OCHRE's architecture. There is no double-counting: the zone air node is inflated, and the furniture/wall nodes add their own distinct mass.

## Recommended Tests

1. **Unit test: zone air capacitance formula** — Assert `derive_zone_capacitances` returns exactly `ρ·Cp·V×7` for a known volume. Existing tests at `boundary_rc.rs:928-963` already cover this. **Coverage: adequate.**

2. **Integration test: thermal time constant with furniture** — Build an isolated zone with only a furniture boundary and a heat injection. Verify the zone temperature time constant τ = R_film × C_furniture is in the expected range (should be significantly longer than air-only). **Coverage: gap — no test verifies furniture contributes to time constant.**

3. **LWR participation test** — Verify that furniture/interior wall `InteriorSurfaceInfo` entries are present in `interior_lwr_zones` after `build_solver_boundaries`. **Coverage: gap — no test checks LWR inclusion of same-zone boundaries.**

4. **Double-count regression** — For a reference building, log and compare the total effective thermal capacitance (zone air + all RC nodes) against OCHRE's state vector capacitances for the same building. Ensures the additive structure hasn't drifted. **Coverage: gap — existing parity tests compare temperatures but not capacitances.**
