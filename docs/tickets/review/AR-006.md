## Background/Context

Energy conservation is the fundamental invariant of a building simulation. If energy
is double-counted (e.g., HVAC heat counted as both delivered energy and internal gain),
zone temperatures drift. If energy is lost (e.g., duct losses not routed to any zone),
the energy balance is wrong. Premature aggregation — summing contributions before they
reach the solver — makes these bugs invisible.

## Investigation Instructions

1. **Trace the full thermal energy path for one heating timestep:**
   - HVAC computes `delivered_heat_w` → split by duct DSE → zone ports
   - Thermal solver receives zone ports → updates state vector
   - What fraction of `delivered_heat_w` actually reaches the zone?
   - Is `1 - DSE` accounted for somewhere? Where does it go?

2. **Check for double counting:**
   - Is HVAC fan heat added to both the electrical port AND the thermal port?
     (It should be in both — electrical for consumption, thermal for zone gain.)
     But is it counted twice in the zone thermal balance?
   - Is HPWH zone extraction counted as both cooling (negative thermal) and as
     equipment electrical consumption? (It should be negative thermal to zone.)
   - Are internal gains from scheduled loads added to zone thermal AND separately
     to a dwelling-level total? If so, is the dwelling total excluding zone gains?

3. **Check for energy losses (sinks with no accounting):**
   - Duct losses when duct zone == conditioned zone (known: discarded in DL-002)
   - Water heater standby losses — do they go to the tank's zone?
   - Ventilation exhaust — does rejected heat go anywhere?
   - EV charging losses — heat dissipated, accounted for?

4. **Trace aggregation points:**
   - Where do per-equipment contributions get summed?
   - After summation, can you still see per-equipment breakdown?
   - Is there a point of no return where disaggregation is impossible?
   - Can output at different verbosity levels recover the breakdown?

5. **Recommend energy balance invariant tests:**
   - `sum(all zone gains) + sum(all losses) = 0` within tolerance
   - `electrical_input - thermal_output - losses = 0` per equipment

## Findings

### Finding 1 — BUG: HVAC fan heat missing from zone thermal port

**Severity: Medium**

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/hvac/furnace.rs:341-368`,
`crates/hares-equipment/src/hvac/heat_pump/heater.rs:814-815`

**OCHRE reference:** `vendors/OCHRE/ochre/Equipment/HVAC.py:543`
```python
self.delivered_heat = heat_gain * self.shr + self.fan_power  # SHR=1 for fan
```

In OCHRE, fan motor heat is explicitly added to `delivered_heat` and therefore to zone
sensible gain. In HARES, fan power is only posted to the electrical port. For a gas
furnace the thermal port receives only `gross_capacity_w = rated_capacity_w * duty`
(furnace.rs:340-368). The `fan_kw` is emitted as electrical consumption only (line 355-360)
and is never added to `write_zone_thermal_contributions`. This means the fan motor heat —
which is dissipated entirely inside the air distribution system — is absent from the zone
energy balance. At typical residential HVAC (1 kW fan on a 10 kW furnace), this is a
~10% undercount of delivered sensible heat to the zone. The same omission applies to
ASHP heating (`heater.rs:814-815`) where `fan_power_w` is included in `electric_kw` but
`thermal_output_w = hp_capacity_w + er_capacity_w` excludes it.

For AC/cooling, the fan shaft heat correction IS applied to the entering wet-bulb
temperature seen by the coil (`air_conditioner.rs:782-801`) as a first-order temperature
correction, but fan motor heat is not separately injected into the zone thermal port.

**Fix required:** For all HVAC equipment that has ducted airflow (furnace, ASHP heater,
AC), fan power should be added to the sensible gain routed to the conditioned zone via
`write_zone_thermal_contributions`. The fraction of fan heat reaching the conditioned
zone should be `dse` (fan is within the duct system and subject to the same losses).

### Finding 2 — BUG: `ThermalCategory::DuctLoss` is defined but never emitted

**Severity: Low (diagnostic accuracy only)**

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/hvac/duct_distribution.rs:70-81`,
`crates/hares-types/src/ports.rs:27-28`,
`crates/hares-envelope/src/thermal_solver/mod.rs:376-378`

The `ThermalCategory::DuctLoss` enum variant exists and is tracked in
`EnvelopeComponentGains.duct_loss_w`, but no equipment ever emits a `PortContribution::Thermal`
with this category. The duct zone entry in `zone_heat_fractions` receives
`ThermalCategory::HvacHeating` or `HvacCooling` rather than `DuctLoss`. As a result,
`EnvelopeComponentGains.duct_loss_w` is always 0 in output, making duct losses invisible
in the component gain breakdown.

**Fix required:** In `write_zone_thermal_contributions`, when posting heat to a zone that
is NOT the conditioned zone (i.e., it is the duct zone or basement zone), the contribution
should be tagged `ThermalCategory::DuctLoss` rather than `HvacHeating`/`HvacCooling`.
This is a diagnostic/disaggregation fix that does not affect the zone state vector.

### Finding 3 — BUG: Duct loss (1 − DSE) discarded when `duct_zone_id == zone_id`

**Severity: Medium (energy loss)**

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/hvac/duct_distribution.rs:35-41`

When `duct_zone_id == zone_id` (ducts in the conditioned space), the `1 − DSE` fraction
of gross capacity is silently dropped. The conditioned zone receives only `dse` × gross
capacity, and the remainder exits the energy balance entirely. The test
`update_zone_heat_fractions_same_duct_zone` (duct_distribution.rs:211-241) explicitly
documents this: "Fractions sum to dse, not 1.0: duct loss (1 − dse) is unrouted."

OCHRE's Python dict assignment (`zone_fractions[self.duct_zone] = 1 - dse`) would
*overwrite* the conditioned zone entry in this edge case — that is a separate OCHRE bug.
However, the physically correct answer when ducts are within the conditioned space is
that DSE = 1.0 (no losses), or that duct zone losses should be credited back to the
conditioned zone (since both are the same space). Discarding ~10–20% of heating capacity
when this configuration occurs causes systematic under-heating.

**Context from prior tickets:** DL-002 previously identified this. The test was added to
document the current behaviour. The behaviour is still incorrect.

**Fix required:** When `duct_zone_id == zone_id`, either force `dse = 1.0` (correct
physical interpretation: ducts in conditioned space have no external losses), or add the
`1 − dse` fraction back to the conditioned zone entry. The first approach is cleaner.

### Finding 4 — GAP: Gas WH standby loss pre-estimate uses nominal UA, not actual node UA

**Severity: Low**

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/water_heater/gas.rs:438-441`,
`crates/hares-equipment/src/water_heater/tank.rs:157-166`

The gas water heater pre-estimates standby loss for zone posting using:
```rust
let standby_loss_w = self.tank.ua_w_per_k() * (avg_temp_before - ambient_c).max(0.0);
```
This uses the nominal `ua_w_per_k` (the base lateral UA). However, `tank.apply_conduction_and_standby`
uses `ua_per_node`, which sums to `ua_w_per_k + 2 × ua_end` (end-cap additions, default
+20% for typical n≥2 node tanks per tank.rs:157-166). The pre-estimate therefore
understates actual tank heat loss by approximately 10–20%. This means the zone receives
less sensible heat than the tank actually surrenders — a small systematic underestimate.

The resistance WH correctly uses `tank.skin_loss_w()` (post-step actual value,
resistance.rs:535). The gas WH pre-estimates before calling `tank.step()` to avoid
computing before/after delta. An alternative is to post the skin loss after `tank.step()`
using `self.tank.skin_loss_w()` multiplied by `skin_loss_fraction`, identical to the
resistance WH pattern.

**Fix required:** Change `gas.rs` standby loss computation to use `self.tank.skin_loss_w()
* self.skin_loss_fraction` after `tank.step()`, consistent with resistance.rs pattern.

### Finding 5 — ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE: Ventilation fan heat not in zone thermal port

**Severity: Acceptable (within ASHRAE modeling convention)**

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/ventilation.rs:334-360`

The ventilation equipment posts `q_sensible_w = m_dot * Cp * (T_supply - T_indoor)` to
the zone thermal port. This is the net sensible load from ventilation airflow and is
correct for the zone energy balance. The fan motor electrical consumption (fan_kw) is
reported to the electrical port only.

Fan waste heat from ventilation motors is a small fraction (~50 W residential) and is
conventionally handled by including it in the building's miscellaneous electrical loads
(internal gains from scheduled loads). OCHRE uses `ScheduledLoad` for ventilation and
does not separately model motor heat injection. The current HARES treatment is acceptable
provided that fan heat does not need to be a separate traceable line item.

### Finding 6 — ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE: HPWH zone heat extraction energy accounting is correct

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/water_heater/heat_pump_wh.rs:775-801`

The HPWH correctly posts a negative (or near-zero) sensible gain to the zone when the
HP is running, representing heat extracted from the zone. The formula:
```rust
let hp_waste_w = compressor_power_w - delivered_hp_w;  // negative when COP > 1
let sensible_gain_w = (hp_waste_w * shr + fan_parasitic_w + er_waste_w) * keep_fraction;
```
When COP > 1 (normal operation), `hp_waste_w < 0`, so the zone receives a net cooling
effect equal to `(compressor_power_w - delivered_hp_w) * shr`. The `fan_parasitic_w` and
`er_waste_w` add back positive gains. The electrical consumption posted separately to the
electrical port is the full compressor+fan+ER power. There is no double-counting: electrical
consumption and negative zone thermal contribution are causally related but measured in
different domains.

---

### Finding 7 — ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE: EV charging losses do not reach conditioned zone

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/ev/mod.rs:724-728`

EV charging waste heat (`ohmic_like_heat_w`) heats the battery thermal mass only. No
`PortContribution::Thermal` is emitted. This is physically correct: EVs are typically
parked outdoors or in unconditioned garages. The battery temperature model is internal
to the EV model. If future configurations place EVs in conditioned spaces (e.g., indoor
charging stations), a zone thermal port would need to be added.

---

### Finding 8 — GAP: No energy balance invariant enforcement at dwelling level

**Files:** `crates/hares-core/src/dwelling/mod.rs:2353-2400` (check_invariants),
`crates/hares-core/src/invariants.rs`

The dwelling has an invariant checker (`check_invariants`) but no test verifies that
`sum(all zone thermal ports) + sum(all unaccounted losses) ≈ sum(all energy inputs)` at
each timestep. The invariant checker currently checks electrical balance but there is no
documented thermal balance check. This gap means that Findings 1, 3, and 4 could exist
for extended periods without automatic detection.

---

### Finding 9 — ACCEPTABLE DIFFERENCE: Aggregation is irreversible at zone level

**Files:** `crates/hares-envelope/src/thermal_solver/ports.rs:13-23`,
`crates/hares-envelope/src/thermal_solver/mod.rs:363-401`

`ThermalAccumulator` accumulates per-category sensible gains per zone from all equipment.
Once `apply_port_sensible_inputs` maps them into the state-space input vector `u`, the
per-equipment breakdown is gone — only per-category per-zone totals are preserved in
`EnvelopeComponentGains`. This is the intended design. Per-equipment thermal breakdowns
are available via the `observe` feature's `EquipmentObservation` diffs (dwelling/mod.rs
observer capture). The `component_gains` accessor preserves enough resolution for OCHRE
parity output columns (`Internal Heat Gain - Indoor (W)`, `HVAC Heating Delivered (W)`,
etc.).

### Finding 10 — GAP: Resistance WH 100% skin loss vs. gas WH partial skin loss inconsistency

**Severity: Low**

**Files:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/water_heater/resistance.rs:534-544`,
`crates/hares-equipment/src/water_heater/gas.rs:440-441`

The resistance WH posts `skin_loss_w` (the full tank-side UA loss) to the zone with no
`skin_loss_fraction` reduction. The gas WH multiplies by `skin_loss_fraction` (default
0.91 for typical AFUE). This represents the real physical difference (gas WH has flue
losses that remove some heat from the tank exterior), but the resistance WH lacks an
equivalent configurable fraction. An argument can be made that a resistance WH in an
insulated utility closet should also not send 100% of jacket losses to the conditioned
zone. However this mirrors OCHRE's behavior and may be intentional. Note for review.

## Thermal Energy Path Summary

For a gas furnace heating timestep (DSE < 1, duct zone ≠ conditioned zone):

1. `gross_capacity_w = rated_capacity_w × duty` (furnace.rs:340)
2. `zone_heat_fractions`: conditioned zone = `dse × (1 − basement_frac)`,
   duct zone = `1 − dse` (duct_distribution.rs:19-41)
3. `write_zone_thermal_contributions(ports, gross_capacity_w, ...)` distributes
   gross × fraction to each zone as `HvacHeating` (duct_distribution.rs:44-82)
4. `apply_port_sensible_inputs(u, ports)` accumulates sensible gains into state-space
   input vector (thermal_solver/ports.rs:14-22)
5. CN step advances zone temperature (thermal_solver/stepping.rs:66-182)

Fan heat (step 1 above) is absent from steps 2-5. The fan kW is only in the electrical
port. This is Finding 1.

## Recommended Tests

### Test 1 — Furnace zone thermal balance includes fan heat
In a single-zone no-duct (DSE=1) heating scenario, assert:
```
zone_sensible_port_w ≈ rated_capacity_w × duty + fan_power_w × duty
```
Currently this assertion will fail, confirming Finding 1.
**File:** `crates/hares-equipment/tests/hvac_tests.rs` (new test)

### Test 2 — Duct zone receives `ThermalCategory::DuctLoss`, not `HvacHeating`
With DSE < 1 and a distinct duct zone, assert:
```
duct_zone_thermal.sensible_for_category(DuctLoss) > 0
duct_zone_thermal.sensible_for_category(HvacHeating) == 0
```
Currently both assertions will fail. Confirms Finding 2.
**File:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/hvac/duct_distribution.rs` tests section

### Test 3 — Duct zone == conditioned zone should yield full gross capacity
With DSE=0.85 and `duct_zone_id == zone_id`, assert conditioned zone receives
`gross_capacity_w` (not `gross_capacity_w × 0.85`). Currently `(1 − 0.85) × gross`
is discarded. Confirms Finding 3.
**File:** `crates/hares-equipment/src/hvac/duct_distribution.rs` test section

### Test 4 — Gas WH standby loss to zone matches post-step `skin_loss_w`
With a known tank temperature and ambient, assert that the sensible gain posted to the
zone thermal port equals `tank.skin_loss_w() × skin_loss_fraction` rather than the
pre-estimate using nominal UA. Confirms and quantifies Finding 4.
**File:** `crates/hares-equipment/tests/water_heater_tests.rs`

### Test 5 — Dwelling-level thermal energy balance integration test
Run a single timestep with a known configuration (furnace only, no infiltration,
adiabatic envelope). Assert:
```
|hvac_heating_w - delta_zone_energy / dt| < tolerance
```
where `delta_zone_energy` = change in zone thermal mass energy. A persistent imbalance
signals unaccounted fan heat or other discrepancies.
**File:** `tests/conditioned_oracle.rs` or `crates/hares-core/tests/engine.rs`
